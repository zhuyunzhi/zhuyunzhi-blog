How it runs

Four steps · plain and simple

  1. You write, briefly

    Two or three lines: what you make, who buys it now, and what you would like to be true in a year.

  2. I read it properly

    Not instantly — this is not a support desk. But it does get read, and it gets read properly.

  3. One call, or a straight no

    If it is something I can genuinely help with, we talk once. If it is not, you get a no rather than a slow silence.

  4. Whatever comes of it, you keep

    Nothing is decided up front, and nothing is expected afterwards. If the conversation is useful, you act on it in your own time.

Good fit, bad fit

Read this before writing
Works well when
    You already make something real, and there is a shipping record to prove it. You want a peer's read rather than a supplier's pitch. Someone on your side can act on the answer without hiring anyone.
Look elsewhere when
    You need something done this week. This is not a service desk. You want a guarantee of enquiry volume. Nobody can promise you the market. You are looking for an agent, a buyer's representative, or someone to negotiate on your behalf.
